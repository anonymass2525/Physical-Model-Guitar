# Physical Model Guitar 1.1 Research — Unified Contact/Waveguide Study

## What is actually implemented

- 6 strings × 2 transverse travelling-wave polarizations, physical positive port impedance Z in N·s/m and 2-location modal bridge from v1.0.
- New energy-accounted finite-duration pick contact: initial spring energy is supplied by the plucker and scattered into the wave at the pluck position; residual contact energy is dissipated on detachment.
- New unilateral fret contacts: displacement estimate, bounded reflection, restitution loss. Contact geometry is a **low-order approximation**, not a waveguide spatial discretization of all fret crowns.
- Reduced-order axial oscillator (two quadrature states) exchanges energy with both transverse planes via orthogonal rotations; magnitude-dependent, energy-preserving allpass phase adjustment approximates tension stiffening. **Not** a full longitudinal PDE.
- Velocity pickup observation taps different waveguide positions without feeding any energy back to the string; detailed pickup coil electrodynamics remains future research.
- `physics11` ON/OFF switches only these new elements; `physics10=ON, physics11=OFF` reproduces the v1.0 Research audio **bit for bit** at matched settings. v0.9 dry/contact pathway remains parallel and **outside** the new subsystem energy ledger.

## Energy budget

`injectedEnergy (J) = string+axial+pick state energy (J) + bridge modal stored energy (J) + bridge damping (J) + propagation losses (J) + pick/fret contact losses (J) + axial damping (J) + stopped-string mute losses (J)`.

11-state local scattering uses orthogonal 2x2 state rotations so source/sink accounting is explicit. Fret restitution is |r|<=1. The read-only pickup reports no active force. This identity is limited to the **new waveguide plus bridge and local interactions** and is *not* a total acoustic/electromagnetic conservation theorem for the complete legacy guitar plugin.

## Further required work

The string is still a single roundtrip traveling-wave buffer; a physically localized two-direction staggered mesh and consistent nonlinear finger/fret contact in displacement/velocity remains future work. Calibrate pick stiffness and actual fret clearance from matched multi-take recordings, measure string tension/mass density, and implement a force-coupled electromagnetic pickup model before claiming complete physical identification.
